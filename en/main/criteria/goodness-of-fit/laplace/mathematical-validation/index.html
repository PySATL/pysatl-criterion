    Laplace: mathematical validation¶

    Scope and sources¶

    The catalog contains 38 concrete criteria, each with a separate page: 13 generated EDF criteria, five generated frequency criteria, Greenwood and 19 fitted specialist criteria. The original 19 use a fully specified Laplace(t, s) null. The added 19 use a composite location-scale null; see the fitted-test audit below. The checks below distinguish independent numerical validation from bibliographic attribution. No Laplace-specific power or optimality claim is made.

    The definitions of D, W², A², V and U² follow Section 2 and Case 0 of Stephens (1974), EDF Statistics for Goodness of Fit and Some Comparisons. Greenwood uses the n+1 spacings, including both endpoints, in equations (1)–(2) of Does, Helmers and Klaassen (1988). These sources concern continuous, fully specified null distributions. No Laplace-specific power or optimality claim is made.

    Formulas and independent checks¶

    Let z=(x-t)/s, F(x)=exp(z)/2 for z<0 and F(x)=1-exp(-z)/2 otherwise. Let u_i=F(x_(i)), i=1,…,n, with u_0=0, u_(n+1)=1. The original six statistics use the unmodified, unscaled definitions below. Scaled and corrected Kuiper variants are listed separately.

    Code Formula Independent verification
    KS D=max(D+,D−), D+=max(i/n−u_i), D−=max(u_i−(i−1)/n) SciPy CDF and direct maxima; all three directions
    CVM W²=1/(12n)+Σ(u_i−(2i−1)/(2n))² SciPy probabilities and numerical integration of n(F_n(u)−u)²
    AD A²=−n−Σ((2i−1)/n)(log u_i+log(1−u_(n+1−i))) SciPy log probabilities, weighted numerical integration, analytic extreme-tail limits
    KUI V=D++D− Direct maxima using SciPy probabilities
    WAT U²=W²−n(mean(u)−½)² Direct formula and integration of centered EDF discrepancy
    GRW G=Σ(u_i−u_(i−1))², i=1,…,n+1 Direct spacings including endpoints and exact tied-sample example G=¼

    Additional EDF criteria¶

    Use the same sorted probabilities and define r_i=i−½. Each short code below has the suffix _LAPLACE_GOODNESS_OF_FIT in the registry.

    Code and page Formula Independent verification
    KS_PLUS D+=max(i/n−u_i) Direct maximum of SciPy Laplace probabilities
    KS_MINUS D−=max(u_i−(i−1)/n) Direct maximum of SciPy Laplace probabilities
    KUIPER_SCALED √n(D++D−) Direct scaling of independent maxima
    KUIPER_CORRECTED (√n+0.155+0.24/√n)(D++D−) Direct scaling of independent maxima
    LIAO_SHIMOKAWA Σ max(i/n−u_i, u_i−(i−1)/n)/√(u_i(1−u_i))/√n Direct weighted formula using SciPy probabilities
    ZHANG_ZK max[r_i log(r_i/(nu_i))+(n−r_i)log((n−r_i)/(n(1−u_i)))] Direct likelihood-ratio expression
    ZHANG_ZA −Σ[log(u_i)/(n−r_i)+log(1−u_i)/r_i] Direct logarithmic expression
    ZHANG_ZC Σ[log((1−u_i)/u_i)−log((n−i+¼)/(i−¾))]² Direct log-odds expression

    The added pages retain the shared algorithms' scientific references: Smirnov for directional KS, Stephens/Lemeshko for Kuiper scaling, Zhang (2002), equations (3.1)–(3.3), for Z_K/Z_A/Z_C, and Liao–Shimokawa (1999). The latter is an adaptation to the fixed Laplace null; fitted-Weibull tables from that paper do not apply. The additional references are inherited from the shared algorithms' documentation; their full texts were not independently reverified during this documentation update (the DOI retrievals were unavailable). The direct-formula tests verify implementation, not published power claims.

    Frequency criteria¶

    Let O_j be observed counts and E_j=np_j>0 expected counts for exhaustive bins. With equal-probability bins E_j=n/m. Explicit or adaptive strategies can have unequal expected counts. For each of the five classes, independent counts (2,3,4,5) and equal expected counts are compared with SciPy power_divergence.

    Code and page Formula SciPy λ Empty observed bin
    CHI2_PEARSON Σ(O_j−E_j)²/E_j 1 contributes E_j
    FREEMAN_TUKEY 4Σ(√O_j−√E_j)² −½ contributes 4E_j
    MODIFIED_LOG_LIKELIHOOD 2Σ E_j log(E_j/O_j) −1 +infinity
    CHI2_NEYMAN Σ(O_j−E_j)²/O_j −2 +infinity
    CRESSIE_READ (9/5)Σ O_j[(O_j/E_j)^(⅔)−1] ⅔ zero contribution in this expression

    These are the shared power-divergence definitions documented with the Pearson and Cressie–Read references on the criterion pages. Cressie–Read fixes λ=⅔; there is no lambda_ constructor option. Modified log likelihood uses E_j in the weight and differs from the ordinary G statistic (λ=0).

    Extreme-value checks for the original six criteria¶

    For (-a,0,a), A² approaches 2a/3+8log(2)/3−3 as a grows. At a=10^308 the result remains representable. This detects premature overflow when tail logs are added before normalization. Watson at n=1 equals 1/12 for every observation; equally spaced midpoint probabilities give 1/(12n). Location-scale transformations are checked independently for all six criteria.

    Changes and compatibility¶

    • Converted continuous/laplace.py into a package with explicit exports and a generated __init__.pyi; generator supports --check without importing the package.
    • Preserved all six public class names, short codes and the continuous.laplace module identity used by pickle. The abstract family no longer declares hypotheses; concrete classes and factory specifications declare both parameters fixed.
    • All 13 EDF and five frequency criteria use shared factories and kernels. Greenwood is the only manually declared concrete class. Generated KS retains its existing constructor through the factory compatibility option; its former call to the removed multi-argument KS interface is corrected.
    • do_execute_statistic(sample) remains an observation-based compatibility entry point. Private _calculate_statistic helpers were removed; benchmark scripts now use the public execute_statistic API. mode remains a compatibility setting.
    • Analytic CDF and log probabilities and Greenwood spacings are static cached Numba methods. No numerical helpers remain outside classes.
    • Shared AD log evaluation now weights each tail before adding, avoiding intermediate overflow. The mathematical definition and calibration scale are unchanged.
    • A factory validation callback retains Laplace's rejection of strings and other nonnumeric inputs. Masked observations are now rejected instead of silently used.

    Input and calibration limits¶

    Nonempty one-dimensional finite real samples are required. Singletons, ties, constant samples and gaps of any width are allowed. The support is the entire real line. EDF and Greenwood require no binning; ties give zero spacings. Frequency criteria use a configurable binning strategy and positive expected counts. Empty observed bins are handled as specified in the table above. The exact continuous null calibration assumes continuous observations; rounding-induced ties require a matching observation model in simulation.

    For EDF and Greenwood, the PIT gives a parameter-free null law at fixed n when both parameters are known. Frequency calibration must also match binning, including adaptive bin selection; arbitrary binning is not parameter-free. The default has no minimum expected-count constraint, so a chi-squared approximation is not automatically justified. See binning calibration. Monte Carlo must call the same statistic, including the KS direction. Stored KS calibration is rejected for LEFT/RIGHT because its key does not encode direction. Fitting t or s outside this API does not justify using the known-parameter null law; a fitted test would need a separately supported hypothesis and refitting in every simulation. All 19 criteria use the right rejection tail. Greenwood's right-tail form detects clustering; universal consistency is not asserted.

    CDF rounding can erase tiny tail spacings. AD, Liao–Shimokawa and Zhang K/A/C use analytic log tails instead of reconstructing rounded tail probabilities. Standardization avoids overflow in x−t when the quotient is representable; a result beyond float64 range is still +infinity. No clipping or Python high-precision fallback is used. Warm timing includes validation, sorting, CDF evaluation and the shared kernel.

    Documentation coverage¶

    All 19 concrete classes have individual pages linked from the catalog and MkDocs navigation. Each page states the fixed null, formula, input limits, rejection direction, calibration requirements, an executable example and scientific sources. mathematical-validation.md summarizes verification; it does not replace those pages.

    Fitted-test audit¶

    The added formulas follow the original article citations in each class and Desgagné–Lafaye de Micheaux–Ouimet, comparison, Section 3. Exact definitions record all estimator and normalization choices.

    Independent checks in tests/statistics/test_laplace_published.py cover:

    • Meintanis: numerical integration of the squared empirical-characteristic-function discrepancy, both weights and both parameter estimators.
    • Choi–Kim: direct spacings, van Es harmonic correction and independent regression slopes for Correa.
    • AP: entropy spacings and numerical integration of the absolute deviation of the reconstructed piecewise-linear quantile; corrected and MLE variants.
    • Gel, GV and rescaled V3/V4: direct sample central moments and the correct centering of absolute deviations.
    • A_rat: direct products over every admissible integer window; optional log transform checked separately.
    • AJ: independent SciPy fitted CDF differences.
    • DLO: even/odd corrections checked at n=4, 5, 20, 21, including the zero-log-zero convention.
    • Data-driven smooth: independent population integrals in observation coordinates, covariance solve and Schwarz selection.
    • All added classes: hypothesis capabilities, registry/pickle, affine invariance, permutation invariance, finite-real input validation, no sample mutation and calibration sampler resolution.

    Explicit adaptations and limits¶

    Entropy windows default to m=1 rather than silently extrapolating power-optimal tables. AP_y rejects ties; other spacing tests use their limiting zero/infinite statistic when a required spacing vanishes. A_rat uses the additional n/2 upper window bound from the comparison, and offers an explicit log-scale option. DLO starts at n=4 because its finite-sample odd-n variance correction is invalid at n=3.

    The smooth moment components are rescaled to asymptotic unit variance (63 and 1188). Their reconstruction uses the moment components and variances reported in Batsidis, Economou and Bar-Lev (2022), Section 3.3, and is independently checked using Laplace moments. The original Best et al. full text was unavailable; no unverified DD formula is attributed to it. The data-driven class is explicitly a Laplace specialization of the general Janic–Ledwina score construction, not a replication of the DD comparator in Best et al.

    Most arithmetic runs in cached nopython kernels. Meintanis has a quadrature fallback for cancellation/extreme weights. Data-driven population matrices are computed once per instance with SciPy, outside the sample kernel. Fitting/validation remains NumPy.
