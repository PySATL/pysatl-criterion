    Mathematical verification of log-normal statistics¶

    Audit date: 2026-10-17. Scope: all 55 concrete classes exported by statistics.goodness_of_fit.continuous.log_normal: 15 EDF variants, five frequency statistics, one spacing statistic, 32 logarithmic normality wrappers and two KL functionals. The catalog lists every class, full code, hypothesis, rejection tail and individual documentation page.

    Findings and corrections¶

    1. Shape tests were presented as tests of fixed parameter values. Normality statistics on logs and both KL statistics eliminate log-location and log-scale. They now accept an empty hypothesis in either coordinate system; Monte Carlo sampling explicitly selects mu=0,s=1. Fully fixed schemas remain accepted for compatibility and remain in hypothesis(), but these shape statistics are invariant to those values. They cannot distinguish two log-normal distributions with different parameters. The 21 fixed-CDF/spacing/frequency tests continue to require both parameters. This is an explicit contract distinction, not implicit parameter filling or a claim that every shape test is omnibus.
    2. Invalid logarithmic data could become a rejection-tail error. A universal +inf penalty is inappropriate for lower-tail tests such as Shapiro–Wilk and Filliben. Logarithmic wrappers and KL now raise ValueError for nonpositive input. Empty, multidimensional, nonfinite, complex and actively masked data are rejected before conversion. Quesenberry–Miller now validates its input too. EDF and frequency behavior follows their mathematical support/zero-count limits.
    3. KL powers and cancellations were unstable. Forming X**(1/sigma_hat) could overflow solely because the measurement unit changed. KL now uses centered, standardized logs, log-sum-exp and a near-zero cumulant series. The former near-zero expression used uncentered log moments and the wrong coefficient; the squared limiting integrand is m3²/6, not its former expression. No integration warnings are suppressed, and zero dispersion is rejected.
    4. The general-alpha KL threshold regression could return impossible negative thresholds. For example, the old supremum expression at n=50, alpha=0.025 gave approximately -103.91 although the statistic is nonnegative. That branch now raises NotImplementedError. The historical alpha=0.01/0.05 formulas remain available for compatibility, explicitly labelled unverified regressions. Their finite-sample level and published domain are not certified by this audit. Nonpositive or nonfinite regression results now raise ValueError too.
    5. Documentation coverage was incomplete. There were 40 pages for 55 classes, without a complete catalog or a log-normal validation report. The pages now state formulas, hypotheses, tails, input restrictions, executable examples and scientific references; all 15 missing pages and MkDocs entries were added. References to the implementation itself were replaced by scientific sources.
    6. Shared kernels and normality definitions were retained. Direct CDF evaluation, KS directions, Zhang–Wu's 10*Z_A-32 normalization and frequency zero-count limits passed independent comparisons. Qualifications for Hosking, GLB and approximate normal-score methods are retained, not silently certified.

    Mathematical reduction and hypothesis conventions¶

    For X>0, log(X) ~ N(mu,s²) if and only if X is log-normal. There is no density Jacobian in a test statistic evaluated on transformed observations: the normality functional is simply T(log(X_1),...,log(X_n)). For location-scale-invariant T, T(a+b*log(X))=T(log(X)) for b>0. This proves the transfer of the implemented functional and its null distribution, but does not prove consistency or verify the scientific provenance of a legacy T.

    For a fully specified null set u_i=Phi((log(x_(i))-mu)/s). The probability integral transform reduces fixed-CDF EDF and spacing criteria to their uniform functionals. No mean/variance is fitted. In shape statistics the normality functional may use s0 (ddof=0), s1 (ddof=1), robust scales or other estimates; those individual conventions are preserved. They must not be confused with the specified log-normal shape parameter s. scale=exp(mu) is the median; the arithmetic mean of X is exp(mu+s²/2).

    All statistics reject in the tail listed in the catalog. Signed two-tail statistics need two simulated quantiles; symmetry is not assumed. D+ and D− are directional CDF discrepancies but both reject large values. Frequency statistics use O_j and E_j=n*p_j in original sample units; only fixed positive cell probabilities justify the ordinary multinomial k-1 asymptotic reference. Adaptive partitions require calibration of the whole binning procedure.

    KL derivation, numerical definition and limits of verification¶

    Let z_i be centered log observations standardized with ddof=0, K® the empirical log moment-generating function and kappa_k its cumulants. With D®=2K®-rK'®, its power series begins

    \[D(r)=-\frac{\kappa_3r^3}{6}-\frac{\kappa_4r^4}{12}+O(r^5).\]

    The denominator satisfies

    \[\sigma^2(r)=e^{r^2}(r^4-3r^2+4)-(4+r^2) =r^6\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(j+1)^2r^{2j}}{(j+3)!}.\]

    Thus R®=D®/sigma® has opposite one-sided limits when skewness is nonzero, but its absolute value and square have continuous extensions. In particular, R(0+)=-kappa3/sqrt(6), R(0+)²=kappa3²/6. The implementation uses these extensions, not a denominator floor. Centering cancels algebraically: replacing log data by log data+c adds cr to K and c to K', cancelling the explicit r*mean term in the uncentered formula. Positive rescaling cancels through the fitted log SD.

    KL_SUP retains the legacy 1000-point equally spaced grid on [-5,5]; this is only a grid approximation to a finite-window supremum. KL_INT integrates nR®² on [-5,5] with adaptive quadrature, split at zero and absolute/relative tolerance 1e-9. No error bound for omitted infinite tails or missed grid maxima is asserted. Decimal 70-digit direct expressions independently check both signs of r, including ±1e-6, plus the integral; scale tests shift log data by ±600. The cumulant expansion is used through order 10 when |r|max|z|<=0.05.

    Batsidis, Economou and Tzavelas (2016) provides the KL size-weighted symmetry motivation. The publisher abstract and author bibliography were available; the primary full text was not. Consequently this audit verifies the documented truncated functionals, not the exact paper's domain, normalization, critical-value regressions or reported power. The old test comments calling two data values “from the article” cannot serve as independent provenance without a pinpoint source. Those rounded regressions are retained as compatibility checks, not used as the mathematical proof.

    Scientific sources and inherited qualifications¶

    The accessible Zhang (2002) paper, §§2–3 provides the fixed-null EDF expressions and likelihood-ratio ranks. The noncyclic Quesenberry–Miller definition is available in the authors' comparative study, Sreedevi and Kattumannil, §4.1. Original bibliographic links appear in every page. A bibliography entry or an accessible abstract is not claimed to be a full-text formula check.

    For the 32 wrappers, the detailed scientific-source audit and independent functional constructions are in the normality report. Their numerical verification was rerun; inherited limitations remain:

    • Hosking 1–4: L-moments agree with exhaustive order-statistic combinations. The three local rows of centering/variance constants have no established primary-source calibration here and do not shrink as an asymptotic variance sequence. They define local discrepancies; simulate at the actual n.
    • GLB: the fitted beta/order-probability transformation and reversed log weights agree with the documented local expression. Equality to the original Glen–Leemis–Barr test remains unconfirmed; do not use its tables by name alone.
    • Coin, SW, SF, SWRG, correlations and spacings: normal order scores or their covariance are approximated. Coin retains its 4<=n<=2000 restriction. These are not exact finite-sample normal covariance inversions.
    • Hermite/moment and directed shape tests: finite-order moments and directed alternatives do not guarantee power against every nonnormal log distribution.
    • Zhang–Wu variants: historical normalization is retained; the log-normal versions use a fixed CDF rather than fitted normality probabilities.
    • Liao–Shimokawa: a fixed-log-normal application of the general EDF functional; the original extreme-value-family calibration is not transferred.
