    Mathematical verification of normality statistics¶

    Audit date: 2026-10-17. Scope: all 60 concrete classes exported by statistics.goodness_of_fit.continuous.normal, including the generated EDF and frequency classes. The catalog lists the exact Python names, storage codes, hypotheses and rejection tails.

    The audit checks implemented mathematical functionals, numerical behavior, hypothesis contracts and documentation. It does not certify exact finite-sample p-values, universal power or every historical author attribution. In particular, eleven legacy classes (four Hosking, six graph and GLB) have qualifications described below. They remain available under their existing names.

    Findings and changes¶

    • No discrepancy was found between the implemented formulas and the independent numerical definitions on the tested cases. For some legacy classes the independently checked definition is the library's documented functional, rather than a fully verified reconstruction of the original publication.
    • Masked observations were silently converted to ordinary arrays by most normal statistics, exposing the hidden values to the calculation. _normal_sample now rejects observations with an active mask, consistent with frequency tests.
    • The D’Agostino–Pearson page incorrectly documented SkewNormalityGofStatistic, a signed skewness test. It now documents DAPNormalityGofStatistic, the sum of squared skewness and kurtosis transforms, and its upper rejection tail.
    • Fifteen generated criteria had no page. They now have separate pages with formulas, examples, sources and fitted-normal calibration instructions.
    • Scientific context was missing for Hosking, GLB and graph pages. References have been added without claiming that related theory establishes their exact local normality tests. Broken links to normal-statistics-review.md were replaced by links to this report and the catalog.
    • Generated class docstrings now distinguish fixed and fitted hypotheses and include executable examples. Existing finite-sample normalizations, including 10*Z_A-32, have been retained.
    • Storage lookup tests were updated to expect the configuration already included by the current DistributionKey implementation; no storage algorithm changed.

    Hypothesis and scale conventions¶

    Write m_r = mean((x-mean(x))**r), s0=sqrt(m2), s1=sqrt(n*m2/(n-1)), and z0=(x-mean(x))/s0, z1=(x-mean(x))/s1. Changing ddof changes a finite-sample statistic, even if the difference vanishes asymptotically. The tests explicitly retain these distinctions.

    Only KS and CVM test a fully specified normal law; their mean and var must be supplied, and var means variance. The other generated EDF and all frequency criteria estimate the mean and unbiased variance on each sample. Thus D+ and D− generated classes have a fitted hypothesis, whereas legacy two-sided KS has a fixed hypothesis. Lilliefors is the fitted two-sided KS statistic.

    Fifty-two classes have a family hypothesis {}. For location-scale-invariant settings their null can be simulated with N(0,1), repeating all fits. This representative distribution does not fix parameters in the hypothesis. Six graph classes instead fix variance and leave mean unknown. User-supplied frequency bin edges are in original units and can break scale invariance.

    For fitted normal EDF tests, independent uniform CDF values cannot replace refitted normal samples. Neither the known-CDF EDF tables nor the familiar multinomial chi-square law can be transferred automatically. In particular, chi-square(k-3) is not asserted for raw-data estimates with adaptive bins. For two-tail tests simulate both quantiles; a signed statistic's null need not be symmetric. Directional KS deviations are nonnegative and reject in the upper tail.

    Sources and level of verification¶

    The following primary texts were inspected where accessible:

    • Zhang (2002), §§2–3: EDF functionals, including half-rank and quarter-rank corrections. KS here is D, not the squared distance printed in §2.1.
    • Doornik and Hansen, author manuscript, appendix p.7: skewness and conditional-kurtosis transformations; rounded printed examples provide an external numerical check. The journal version is Doornik–Hansen (2008).
    • Bonett and Seier (2002), equations (5)–(6): ddof=0, the factor sqrt(n+2) and the two-sided rule. This is the individual Geary-kurtosis transform, not the paper's further combined test.
    • Bontemps and Meddahi (2005), §§2–3: orthonormal Hermite moments. This implementation uses iid weighting and s1; it does not implement the paper's HAC extension to dependent data.
    • Brys, Hubert and Struyf, author manuscript, §3 and Table 1: the MC-LR quadratic form. The library uses more digits in the constants than the table prints; exact agreement with those additional digits is not claimed.
    • Cabaña and Cabaña (2003), author-posted full text: truncated Hermite processes, fixed truncation five and continuous supremum.
    • Desgagné, Lafaye de Micheaux and Leblanc (2013), author copy: fitted-score approach. Independent quadrature verifies the implemented score means and covariance projection, within the precision of rounded constants.
    • Lemeshko et al. (2014), equations (1)–(3), (6): Kuiper normalizations and Watson centering. These equations alone do not validate calibration after normal parameter fitting.

    Direct scientific references for all 60 statistics appear in the coverage table below; their individual pages provide further bibliographic detail. A bibliographic link alone is not evidence of a line-by-line formula check. Original full texts for several older methods were inaccessible; those methods were checked against independent mathematical constructions or external software, with approximations stated explicitly below.

    External software checks use the official SciPy definitions of Shapiro–Wilk, skewness, kurtosis and Filliben plotting positions. Agreement with a numerical implementation complements, but does not replace, scientific attribution.

    Legacy definitions and unresolved scientific attribution¶

    Hosking 1–4. The L-moment and trimmed L-moment calculations agree with exhaustive averaging of order-statistic combinations. The three historical rows (mu4,v3,v4) for n<=25, 25<n<=50 and n>50 are not an asymptotically shrinking covariance sequence. Neither Hosking (1990) nor Hosking (2007) is claimed to justify these exact rows. These are local discrepancies: simulate at the actual sample size. No unsupported replacement constants were introduced.

    Graph statistics. Exhaustive small-graph checks establish their combinatorial values. The sample-dependent radius range/(10*m2) changes as inverse scale, while interpoint distances change as scale. Hence location invariance holds, but scale invariance does not. Mean can be set to zero for simulation; variance must match the hypothesis. No paper establishing this exact normality procedure was located. Ebner, Nestmann and Schulte is explicitly cited as related graph-testing research about uniformity, not as provenance for these six normality tests.

    GLB. The fitted beta transformation and reversed logarithmic weights are reproduced using binomial probabilities. This does not make the expression the ordinary Anderson–Darling statistic. The Glen–Leemis–Barr paper was identified, but the primary full text was inaccessible. Its exact equality to this fitted-normal implementation remains unconfirmed. Use simulation of the implemented functional, not an assumed GLB table.

    Approximations and directed tests. Coin's regression is checked against quadrature of expected normal order statistics, with tolerance reflecting its nscor2 approximation. Bonett–Seier is a kurtosis component; GMG is directed against heavy tails. Fixed-order Hermite and moment tests are not proved consistent against every nonnormal alternative by this audit. A finite value or Monte Carlo calibration alone does not establish omnibus power.

    Numba optimization verification¶

    Numerical kernels use static class methods decorated with @jit(nopython=True, cache=True), without fast-math reassociation. The public entry points retain input validation and stable rescaling. All 60 criteria reach compiled kernels; generated EDF and frequency criteria reuse their existing common kernels. Normal quantiles and beta/log-tail functions remain in SciPy, and Cabaña polynomial root finding remains in NumPy. Compiling a reduction does not mean the entire public call is compiled.

    The 128 additional checks in test_normal_numba.py exercise every public path on continuous and tied data, require actual nopython signatures and disk-cache configuration, and compare compiled results with the Python kernel bodies. They also check large Hosking combination weights and strict graph-radius boundaries against explicit graph calculations. Independent formula checks listed above remain the mathematical reference. Baseline comparisons before and after optimization also passed on normal samples of sizes 12, 100 and 1000 for all 39 explicitly implemented statistics (rtol=2e-8, atol=2e-9).

    Graph statistics now use sorted scans instead of constructing adjacency lists; the strict edge condition remains abs(x_i-x_j) < radius. BHS uses one flat medcouple-kernel array instead of multiple broadcast matrices, preserving its tie convention. Hosking uses floating-point low-order combination products to avoid fixed-width integer overflow. Coin and Shapiro–Wilk evaluate quantiles in batches, and Cabaña reuses its fixed polynomial basis.

    A local warmed benchmark used the public API, n=200, seed 123 and the minimum of three batches of ten calls, comparing against the pre-optimization source. Observed speedups were approximately 9× for Epps–Pulley, 13–15× for Hosking 2–4, 15× for Jarque–Bera, 41× for Coin and 89× for Shapiro–Wilk. The four graph statistics that previously built adjacency lists improved by about 186–222×. GLB was essentially unchanged (0.98×), since SciPy beta tails dominate it. These are local measurements, not performance guarantees; compilation/cache loading is excluded from these speedup ratios.

    To measure first-call and warmed execution separately for all 60 criteria:

    PYTHONPATH=src .venv/bin/python benchmarks/benchmark_normal_pipeline.py --isolated-imports

    The explicit isolation flag bypasses the unrelated package-import failure described above. Once that failure is fixed, omit the flag. First-call timings include compilation or disk-cache loading, depending on the cache state.

    Like continuous/uniform, the normality implementation is a package:
