    Mathematical verification of Weibull statistics¶

    This review covers the ordinary two-parameter Weibull implementations in continuous/weibull.py: 13 generated EDF classes, 5 generated frequency classes and 15 explicit specialized classes, for a total of 33 concrete classes. Exponentiated Weibull is a separate family. See the criterion catalog for individual pages and public class names.

    Hypothesis and parameter estimation¶

    The null model has zero location and unknown positive shape \(k\) and scale \(\eta\):

    \[ F(x;k,\eta)=1-\exp[-(x/\eta)^k],\qquad x>0. \]

    Construct criteria with WeibullDistributionDescriptor.DEFAULT.parse({}). The generated criteria fit both parameters by maximum likelihood on every call. For ordered observations, write \(z_i=\widehat k(\log X_{(i)}-\log\widehat\eta)\) and \(u_i=1-\exp[-\exp(z_i)]\). The implementation solves the profile score in a rescaled log domain rather than forming large powers of the original sample. The Weibull-to-extreme-value transformation and likelihood equations are given in Krit (2014), §2, equations (1)–(7).

    Samples for generated criteria must be one-dimensional, finite, strictly positive, nonconstant and have at least two observations. Specialized criteria have additional sample-size, spacing or record requirements. Unknown parameters do not imply that every specialized criterion uses MLE: some eliminate log location and scale through correlations, normalized spacings or moments.

    Formula checks¶

    The generated definitions below were compared with the shared kernels and numerical references. The citations identify the discrepancy; their fixed-parameter critical values do not automatically apply after Weibull fitting.

    Criteria Checked definition Scientific source
    Kolmogorov–Smirnov D+, D−, D \(D^+=\max_i(i/n-u_i)\), \(D^-=\max_i(u_i-(i-1)/n)\); \(D=\max(D^+,D^-)\), without squaring or scaling Zhang (2002), §2.1
    Anderson–Darling \(-n-n^{-1}\sum_i(2i-1)[\log u_i+\log(1-u_{n+1-i})]\); no finite-sample multiplier Anderson and Darling (1952); fitted Weibull context: Liao and Shimokawa (1999)
    Cramér–von Mises \(W^2=1/(12n)+\sum_i[u_i-(i-1/2)/n]^2\) Zhang (2002), §2.3
    Watson \(W^2-n(\bar u-1/2)^2\), evaluated using centered deviations Watson (1961)
    Kuiper, scaled Kuiper, corrected Kuiper \(V=D^++D^-\); \(\sqrt n V\); \((\sqrt n+0.155+0.24/\sqrt n)V\) Lemeshko et al. (2014)
    Zhang K, A, C Likelihood-ratio discrepancies with half-ranks; Z_C uses the quarter-rank log-odds correction Zhang (2002), equations (3.1)–(3.3)
    Liao–Shimokawa \(n^{-1/2}\sum_i\max(i/n-u_i,u_i-(i-1)/n)/\sqrt{u_i(1-u_i)}\), using MLEs Liao and Shimokawa (1999)
    Pearson, Freeman–Tukey, modified log-likelihood, Neyman chi-squared, Cressie–Read Power divergences at \(\lambda=1,-1/2,-1,-2,2/3\), respectively, using fitted bin probabilities Cressie and Read (1984)

    For frequency criteria, \(O_j\) is the observed bin count and \(E_j=np_j>0\). The five kernels return, respectively,

    \[ \sum_j\frac{(O_j-E_j)^2}{E_j},\quad 4\sum_j(\sqrt{O_j}-\sqrt{E_j})^2,\quad 2\sum_j E_j\log\frac{E_j}{O_j},\quad \sum_j\frac{(O_j-E_j)^2}{O_j},\quad \frac95\sum_j O_j[(O_j/E_j)^{2/3}-1]. \]

    An empty observed bin contributes \(E_j\) to Pearson, \(4E_j\) to Freeman–Tukey and zero to Cressie–Read. It makes modified log-likelihood and Neyman chi-squared infinite. These limits are part of the kernel definitions.

    Tiku–Singh / TS \(2\sum_{i=1}^{n-2}(n-1-i)g_i/(n-2)\) with approximate expected log-order spacings; both tails Tiku and Singh (1981); functional checked through Sürücü (2008), (3.4)–(3.5); see indexing and approximation qualifications below

    Scope and qualifications¶

    This document separates checking an implemented formula from establishing its historical attribution or its null distribution. It does not establish finite-sample rejection probabilities, nominal significance levels or power. The Shapiro–Brain numerical formula is unchanged; its rejection region was corrected from the left tail to both tails during the follow-up source review.

    Anderson–Darling, Liao–Shimokawa and the Zhang kernels consume log-CDF and log-survival values. This preserves tail information when ordinary CDF values round to zero or one; representable input does not guarantee a finite statistic. LT2/LT3 also use log-domain arithmetic to avoid overflow followed by zero-weight multiplication. The Kuiper correction is the implemented conventional scaling, not a proof of a fitted-Weibull finite-sample p-value approximation.

    All generated criteria reject for large values. Specialized exceptions include REJG (left tail), and Shapiro–Brain, OK and TS (both tails).

    For generated EDF criteria, Monte Carlo calibration can use shape = scale = 1 because of log-location-scale invariance, but every replicate must refit both parameters. For generated frequency criteria, the built-in resolver permits EqualProbabilityBins and rebuilds the bins after each fit. Other binning rules require external calibration with explicit simulation parameters. No automatic chi-squared law with a parameter-count degrees-of-freedom adjustment is claimed.

    MD requires acquisition order or precompressed lower records with counts. Calibration must reproduce record extraction and the requirement of at least two records; the generic unconditional Monte Carlo path is blocked. For other specialized criteria, nondefault execution settings such as m and a must also be preserved during external simulation. Unversioned stored Weibull critical values are rejected by the current storage-calibration guards.

    Numerical verification¶

    On 2026-10-14, tests/statistics/test_weibull_dynamic.py passed 247 tests with four warnings in an isolated package-loading run. It covers:

    • Registry and pickle identity, per-criterion hypotheses, and completeness of all 13 EDF and 5 frequency exports without explicit class duplicates.
    • Independent SciPy Gumbel fits to log observations and reference KS, AD, Cramér–von Mises and Liao–Shimokawa calculations.
    • Shared-kernel comparisons after an independent fit. These verify integration with the family; they are not independent proofs of the shared formulas.
    • Fitted frequency counts and expected probabilities for explicit and equal-probability bins, including a SciPy Pearson reference.
    • Scale and positive-power transformations, repeated fitting without retained estimates, sample immutability, invalid input, and extreme log probabilities.
    • Monte Carlo refitting, unsupported calibration modes and public stub exports.

    The existing tests/distributions/test_weibull.py also contains references for probability-plot correlations, spacing quadrature, moment-covariance integration, Laplace grids, CQ, entropy windows and record likelihood/integration. Its ordinary run failed during collection* on the pre-existing AbstractChi2Statistic import in continuous/beta/beta_statistics.py; those specialized tests are not reported as passing in this review. The isolated 247-test run bypasses continuous/__init__.py and does not establish that normal package initialization works. The test count is a dated execution result, not a claim that every specialized formula has been independently validated.

    Shapiro–Brain follow-up verification¶

    The formula was checked against Shell (2003), equation (9). The implementation retains an explicit adjustment: centering removes the residual coefficient sum caused by rounding, since \(0.6079\cdot0.4228-0.2570=0.00002012\). Our algebra proves invariance under \(X\mapsto cX^d\) for positive \(c,d\). No MLE is used.

    Dedicated checks in tests/statistics/test_shapiro_brain_weibull.py compare the statistic to an independent scalar evaluation, reproduce the published example before centering, check the centered value separately, and test permutation/scale/power invariance, tied samples, the degenerate \(n=2\) case, input validation and the two-tail declaration. These checks establish the implemented calculation, not a finite-sample significance level or power claim.

    The follow-up isolated run passed 268 tests: the 247 generated-family checks plus 21 dedicated Shapiro–Brain checks. The package-initialization limitation described above still applies.

    Kullback–Leibler follow-up verification¶

    The statistic matches the MLE KL functional in the pinned EWGoF 2.2.2 WEDF.test for the same effective entropy window. Its scientific origin is Pérez-Rodríguez, Vaquera-Huerta and Villaseñor-Alva (2009), with Vasicek entropy estimation and Song's KL methodology. The 2009 full text was not available for direct verification; executable formula correspondence uses EWGoF rather than an assertion of identical published tables.

    Our change-of-variable calculation gives minimum Gumbel for \(Y=\log X\), \(\mu=\log\eta\), \(\sigma=1/k\). Therefore \(\log g(z)=z-e^z\) and the returned \(-H-\bar z+\overline{e^z}\) has the correct signs. EWGoF's MLEst starts with negative logs but reflects its standardized output again, giving the same orientation. Reflection and endpoint repetition preserve the entropy sum; the maximum-Gumbel formulation on negative logs is equivalent. At the MLE, \(\overline{e^z}=1\). There is no extra factor \(n\), exponentiation or nonnegativity clipping. Estimated entropy can produce negative KL estimates.

    The local window is an integer \(1\le m<n/2\), defaulting to \(\min(\lfloor\sqrt n\rfloor,\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor)\). EWGoF instead overwrites paramKL with a piecewise rule (\(n=20\): 3 versus our 4), with uncovered boundary sizes falling through to 14. These conventions are not equivalent. The local profile-score solver also differs from EWGoF's bounded optimizer. No executable formula or window change was needed; the documented variant now explicitly states these differences.

    MLE equivariance under positive affine changes of logs proves invariance to Weibull shape and scale at fixed \(m\). Every Monte Carlo replicate must refit both parameters and preserve the window. Custom execution windows require matching external calibration. Zero window gaps give infinity; individual ties with positive window gaps are allowed. The criterion is right-tailed, with no unqualified normal/chi-square calibration or censoring support.
